The Jealousy I Didn't See Coming

— S, founder of I Like It Deep

Last night was a blast — I finally got to go dumpster diving with a girlfriend. I was nervous, but I had to check it off my bucket list. We hit up a few stores, grabbed just a few things. I was really in it for the thrill, and the whole experience was pretty cool.

This morning, I was talking to my friend's dad, just joking around and having some laughs. After they went off to do some car stuff, I sat there cleaning off the things we got, and out of nowhere I felt like crying. Where was that coming from?! I was having a great time — why would I want to cry?

Probably because a little jealousy crept in, watching how she interacts with her dad. I found myself wishing I had a parent like him — someone down to earth, supportive, kind. My parents looked great on paper, but as a kid in their household, it wasn't a healthy environment to grow up in. My experience was different than my sister's. When I've talked to her, she remembers loving, caring parents who supported her. What I remember is bits and pieces — cruel moments burned into my memory. It makes me hate that I was adopted in the first place, some days.

I don't speak to my parents. It's been four or five years, and I honestly don't plan to ever again. That might sound like black-and-white thinking, but even my therapist agrees — keeping the toxic out of my life is what's best for my mental health, so I just do me. When you grow up only knowing certain ways of life, and then have them ripped away, it breaks you. I spend holidays alone. I'm invited to my boyfriend's, but I'd rather isolate. I get a little jealous seeing families show each other love, when I don't have that.

You might think I could just reach out and make amends, but no. In my eyes, they chose to adopt me and make that commitment. Some days I feel like their five-thousand-dollar purchase of some cute Korean baby was a huge mistake. I had a lot of learning issues growing up, trouble concentrating — I think the label was ADD. There was no early intervention offered like kids get now. By fourth grade I was put on medication to help me concentrate. Did it help? Probably not, since I still struggled daily. Learning things never sat well with me — I was too busy daydreaming about whatever boy was cute, or whatever I was planning to do next.

I often feel like if my parents had taken the time to actually get to know the real me, and my struggles, instead of pushing pills on me from grade school through high school, things might have been different. Who knows — there's no changing the past, but I don't think what they were doing was right. I'm not trying to put them on blast for bad parenting, but it wasn't the loving environment I wish I'd had. There was another girl from my church who was adopted too, and I was always so jealous of her — she had caring parents who never pushed her to be something she wasn't.

Who knows what my life in Korea would have looked like. I'm sure my birth mom had her reasons for giving me up, hoping I'd have a better life with whoever ended up taking me in. She was doing what she thought was best, and I can't blame her for that. I can't even really blame the parents who adopted me. If I stay stuck in the past and the hurt, I'll never grow into who I'm supposed to be. As much as I felt really hurt, and grew up not understanding what love actually meant, I'm still responsible as an adult for my own actions and whatever outcomes come from them. Every decision I've made has led me to exactly where I am today.

Right now I'm sitting in a friend's garage, journaling, trying to force myself to keep going. I wish I'd made better decisions in my life, ones that wouldn't have led to so much heartache. I live with this hole in my heart from my own poor decision-making, and it sucks. It consumes me some days and makes it hard to move forward. I have to live in the moment and be strong enough to pick myself back up on the days my heart hurts — even if that means pretending I'm okay when I'm not.

Right now, my chest feels tight. I know I can't keep ignoring the pain and hoping it just goes away on its own. Maybe someday I'll have the courage to actually talk about it with my therapist, or someone. As much as people say "oh my god, I'm so sorry," it never really means anything unless you've lived it — you just don't understand otherwise. Nothing compares to this kind of pain, or how much of a failure you can feel like when you're just expected to keep living life as-is, without something so close to you. I was so naive to think this could never happen to me. I guess it's the biggest lesson I'll learn in this lifetime.
