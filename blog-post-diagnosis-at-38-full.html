At 38, I Finally Got a Name For It

— S, founder of I Like It Deep

At 38, sitting across from the therapist I've been seeing every week, I finally got a name for something I'd been living with my whole life: BPD. I hit every checkmark on the test. Somehow, relief came before anything else did.

There's a specific kind of comfort in a diagnosis. Not because it fixes anything, but because it means you're not just "too much," or "dramatic," or "crazy" — there's an actual name for what's happening inside you, and other people have found their way through it too. Now I'm doing the work of learning how to handle it, one week at a time.

I also live with PMDD, and between the two of them, the highs and lows don't stay quiet inside me — they show up in my relationship. Three years in, with a man who is genuinely amazing, and I still sometimes can't get the right words out. "I'm fine" comes out when I'm anything but. I close off exactly when I should let him in.

One of the harder parts of BPD is learning that your brain will convince you things are true when they aren't. I do this constantly — believing he's pulling away when he isn't, believing I'm about to be left when nothing has actually changed. It's exhausting, for both of us I'm sure, but especially for me, living inside it.

I'm thankful these moments aren't as extreme as they used to be. I think the steadiness of him — him actually staying, actually not leaving — has slowly turned the volume down. Abandonment issues on top of everything else meant I needed proof, over and over, that he wasn't going anywhere. And somehow, three years later, he still hasn't.

I'm not proud of this part, but I'll say it anyway: sometimes I push him to his limits, or push him away entirely, hoping he'll chase me — like that's proof he loves me. Most of the time it doesn't play out the way it does in my head, and I end up apologizing for the version of me that shows up when I'm scared, asking him to forgive me for something I couldn't fully control in the moment.

I didn't grow up in an emotionally stable, safe environment, and I'm only now, slowly, understanding how much that shaped the adult I became. Low self-esteem. Always picturing the worst outcome. Clinging to him like my whole life depends on him staying. And intimacy — real intimacy — terrifies me. I've kept people at arm's length for years because somewhere in me is the belief that if I let someone all the way in, they'll do exactly what everyone before did: leave.

Writing this out is its own kind of exposure therapy. If any of this sounds like your own Tuesday, you're not broken, and you're not too much. You just might not have had the name for it yet either.
