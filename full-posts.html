Learning to Stay When It's Easier to Cut People Off

Living with BPD, and slowly starting to understand it, is a little sad and lonely for me. I used to have so many friends and acquaintances, so many people to talk to. I guess taking myself off social media, and not doing my part as a "friend" by reaching out, is on me too. Friendships have just become exhausting. I still have people I can talk to if I need to, but I've gotten used to being alone lately.

I also have this tendency to cut people off the second we get into a small disagreement. I've had those friendships with girls where we take a break, reconnect, and do the same dance all over again. I sometimes miss those times together, and I thought it was genuine at the time — but my black-and-white thinking sure doesn't help. I'd love to find a middle ground sometimes, and still keep people around even when we disagree, or when something small could be fixed with a simple apology.

I think with everything I've been through, and not being strong enough to actually work through it like a "normal" person, it's made it hard to find real happiness in things. I've got a lot of resentment built up, and the second I try to touch that subject, I get that tingly feeling in my nose right before I cry. I know I need to address the past and learn to forgive myself someday, or I'll just stay stuck. I truly believe I won't ever be the same after what I've been through.

I feel very alone, and unsupported by people I thought were my family. I might be adopted, but those people took on the role of choosing me. I don't see myself ever making up with my parents someday — but if I could forgive them and set myself free, that would be amazing. I understand the viewpoint from one of the journals, about parents only doing what they knew. I can wrap my head around that, to a point. But at the same time, you adopted me. You took on the role of doing what was best for me — even as an adult. I guess coming from an emotionally and physically abusive household, I shouldn't expect real love back.

I admire people my age who don't have mental health issues, who aren't battling themselves daily over whether they're worthy. Maybe someday I'll actually feel what love feels like. As much as I say it, and hear it from my boyfriend, it's just words right now. Maybe it's the hidden expectations we put on people — in my mind, if this is love, why does it hurt?

That's a lot of rambling all at once. I'm excited to try this DBT class. I have to remind myself I'm doing this for me. I've felt stuck lately, not doing much — no real job, just "taking the time for Stephanie." It has to benefit my future somehow, and if it doesn't, at least I can say I tried.

The Split I Didn't See Coming

This week is sort of a big deal — I was referred to a DBT class. Tomorrow I go in for a three-hour interview to see if I'm a good fit. After checking every box for Borderline Personality Disorder, I want to do what it takes to manage my emotions. It's crazy because I can feel that crazy brewing inside me. I know what I'm thinking isn't always true, yet I'm stuck on stupid trying to make it true. It's not fun.

I just went through an episode last weekend. I have contact with my current boyfriend daily. There are times he needs a break, and I know I have to give him space after I've snapped. But this time was different. I saw him earlier that day, and afterward he was doing a side job — I was expecting to hear from him once he was done, like usual. As the night went on, I didn't hear anything. I sent messages, called — no response. I couldn't sit at home by myself, so I went out to the bar with a girlfriend to take my mind off it. It irritated me the entire night. I was expecting some response, but I didn't hear from him until late the next morning.

That was unacceptable to me — the lack of communication. Right away my mind went to the worst possible idea, and I was livid. He didn't understand it, but I made sure it was obvious how much it hurt me. Inside, I wanted him to hurt like he hurt me. I cried endless tears throughout the day with him, including trying to be as standoffish and short with him as I could. I finally voiced things like, "Do you even understand where I'm coming from and why I'm crying?" He said he got it — it was because he didn't respond, and he didn't think it was that big of a deal. Looking back, I was over the top about it, when I simply could have used my words to communicate everything. I'm hoping he won't do the same thing again, or I'll feel really stupid for letting it go.

Unfortunately, sex was able to "cure" my issues. I've heard people on TikTok talk about how sex can make you feel closer to someone and get confused with love — I completely agree. I've always been super sexual, and in my mind, sex cures everything. With this man, he reminds me that "sex isn't the most important thing in our relationship" when I'm in the mood and he isn't. It drives me bonkers, but sex is important to me — I want it like four times a day. I think sometimes he punishes me by not letting me get on it, lol. But if I feel like I'm in outer space thanks to that man, I'm obsessed, and he best never try breaking up with me.

Back to my original excitement — I hopefully will be starting this DBT class to help manage my heightened emotions. I'm very aware I go above and beyond and only see black and white during my "splits," as people on TikTok call it. I'd like to not be so heated and over-the-top emotional, and possibly not stress out my boyfriend so much. I'm beyond thankful he handles me and tolerates my behavior. I love him, and with my BPD, he is my "favorite person." The times he tries to pull away, I lose my shit — and I mean lose it. What they write about people with BPD, I hit that level. It's embarrassing after the fact. I feel so regretful and ashamed I let myself hit such a low.

I guess I'll keep you updated on how it goes, and my journey to better myself.

July 31, 2026

At 38, I Finally Got a Name For It

At 38, sitting across from the therapist I've been seeing every week, I finally got a name for something I'd been living with my whole life: BPD. I hit every checkmark on the test. Somehow, relief came before anything else did.

There's a specific kind of comfort in a diagnosis. Not because it fixes anything, but because it means you're not just "too much," or "dramatic," or "crazy" — there's an actual name for what's happening inside you, and other people have found their way through it too. Now I'm doing the work of learning how to handle it, one week at a time.

I also live with PMDD, and between the two of them, the highs and lows don't stay quiet inside me — they show up in my relationship. Three years in, with a man who is genuinely amazing, and I still sometimes can't get the right words out. "I'm fine" comes out when I'm anything but. I close off exactly when I should let him in — not because I want to, but because that's what fear does when it's had the wheel for this long. I'm learning, slowly, that being known is safer than I think it is.
