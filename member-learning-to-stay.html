Learning to Stay When It's Easier to Cut People Off

— S, founder of I Like It Deep

Living with BPD, and slowly starting to understand it, is a little sad and lonely for me. I used to have so many friends and acquaintances, so many people to talk to. I guess taking myself off social media, and not doing my part as a "friend" by reaching out, is on me too. Friendships have just become exhausting. I still have people I can talk to if I need to, but I've gotten used to being alone lately.

I also have this tendency to cut people off the second we get into a small disagreement. I've had those friendships with girls where we take a break, reconnect, and do the same dance all over again. I sometimes miss those times together, and I thought it was genuine at the time — but my black-and-white thinking sure doesn't help. I'd love to find a middle ground sometimes, and still keep people around even when we disagree, or when something small could be fixed with a simple apology.

I think with everything I've been through, and not being strong enough to actually work through it like a "normal" person, it's made it hard to find real happiness in things. I've got a lot of resentment built up, and the second I try to touch that subject, I get that tingly feeling in my nose right before I cry. I know I need to address the past and learn to forgive myself someday, or I'll just stay stuck. I truly believe I won't ever be the same after what I've been through.

I feel very alone, and unsupported by people I thought were my family. I might be adopted, but those people took on the role of choosing me. I don't see myself ever making up with my parents someday — but if I could forgive them and set myself free, that would be amazing. I understand the viewpoint from one of the journals, about parents only doing what they knew. I can wrap my head around that, to a point. But at the same time, you adopted me. You took on the role of doing what was best for me — even as an adult. I guess coming from an emotionally and physically abusive household, I shouldn't expect real love back.

I admire people my age who don't have mental health issues, who aren't battling themselves daily over whether they're worthy. Maybe someday I'll actually feel what love feels like. As much as I say it, and hear it from my boyfriend, it's just words right now. Maybe it's the hidden expectations we put on people — in my mind, if this is love, why does it hurt?

That's a lot of rambling all at once. I'm excited to try this DBT class. I have to remind myself I'm doing this for me. I've felt stuck lately, not doing much — no real job, just "taking the time for Stephanie." It has to benefit my future somehow, and if it doesn't, at least I can say I tried.
