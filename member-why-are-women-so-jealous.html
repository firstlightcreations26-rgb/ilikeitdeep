Why Are Women Such Jealous, Dumb Bitches?

— S, founder of I Like It Deep

This weekend I was invited out by my older girlfriend (57), who asked her friend (39) to come join us. I'd met this girl my age before — she's chill and down to earth. During our time together, all three of us, the older friend started getting a little jealous.

I bit my tongue when she made comments, because I know myself, and if I said something it would come out sideways and ruin the friendship. I ignored it — my personality is more dominant than hers, and I say exactly how it is. She wouldn't want to hear it. Out of respect for everyone, I let it slide. I'm very open and brutally honest at times, and I don't tolerate much disrespect, but this was a weird situation.

Our older friend threw her fit and ended up leaving, because she wanted that other girl to herself, basically. It was pure selfishness. I kept thinking — wouldn't she rather we all got along than disliked each other? I was genuinely confused, and a little taken aback that someone older than us would act like such a child.

I have my moments too. I've been on the other end of this — I introduced two girlfriends before, and yes, I'll admit I got jealous when they started hanging out, especially when I wasn't included. But that's just how life goes. Some people click more than others. I get where she's coming from, but you either go with the flow or let it go. You don't get upset and start making snarky comments when you're the one who brought everyone together in the first place. It's frustrating, and honestly a little immature.

I remember when two of my friends were getting close, and one started acting like she knew everything about the girl she'd just met — meanwhile I'd been friends with her for years and already knew all of it. It made my eyes roll, but I let her have her moment and feel special. It does me no good to act like I'm better than her for knowing someone longer. Just silly stuff not worth the energy.

I just wish people would be more honest and vocal about how they feel. Using those "I" statements we all learned about — "I feel left out," "I feel hurt" — simple ways to ease into a hard conversation, instead of throwing shade and making snarky comments. Childish stuff like that has a way of turning into something worse if it never gets said out loud.
