Is It Being Content or Lazy?

— S, founder of I Like It Deep

I'm a little confused about where I'm at right now. Writing this at 7:34pm, I'm happy. I feel great. I didn't accomplish anything today that was impressive, but I feel good. There were even a few things that happened that made me irritated and frustrated, and they're not even phasing me right now, because I'm just living in the moment. I truly have so much to be grateful for — even when it's not even that much, it's still something. I haven't reached for any of the goals I've set, haven't done much of anything really, but I feel light. My depression is being suppressed by this warm, nice feeling.

It might have come from sitting outside and grounding myself. I was brushing my boyfriend's German Shepherd, and while he was laying next to me, I closed my eyes and visualized our paws and feet connected, going deep into the earth, with a golden light coming down from the universe and filling both our bodies. I did that three times, and all three times, this silly guy rolled onto his back with all four legs facing the sky. It was pretty cool watching his reaction. There's gotta be some feeling he picked up on.

I love dogs. I could snuggle one all day and all night. I've always felt a bond with them, just love them completely. Growing up, this guy would find me hanging out in his garage because I'd sneak in through the gate connected to his house just to play with his dog, Tucker. He was a black lab mix and just perfect. I adored him. He was my first love that wasn't officially mine — until I moved out and was finally able to buy my own first true love, Owen. He was a Pomeranian/Yorkie, and this handsome boy was the love of my life. He came with me everywhere. I mean everywhere — to work, on dates when I was meeting a guy, to the store. That dog was by my side for all of it.

I never put any real training into him, since he was my first dog. And even working with dogs daily, having your own was just different. He was spoiled rotten and came before anyone else in my life. I remember boyfriends getting jealous over how I would talk to Owen — full baby talk, all sweet, and not a bit of that for them. Little pussies. That dog was my whole world. He lived to about 10 or 11.

I took him to the vet one day when he was acting goofy, and while we were waiting, he threw up black tar all over me. It broke my heart having to put him down. I saved a part of his beautiful tail and had his paw prints made in clay. I still miss that little guy to this day. I hope he comes back to me someday, in another dog. He's in heaven now with his brother Oscar (a mutt), his step-sister Kitty (a pitbull), and their distant cousin Sampson (a Scottish Fold). All of my babies were amazing pets. I wish they'd lived longer — saying goodbye to every one of them was the hardest thing I've ever had to do. They touched my heart in ways no human ever has.

The day I can finally afford another dog, my next breed is going to be a bull terrier. I don't know what it is about that breed, but I just love them.
